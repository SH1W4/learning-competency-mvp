# LASTRO — Public Product Thesis

## Current thesis

LASTRO investigates how a defined capability claim can be supported by observable evidence, evaluated under explicit criteria, resolved into a bounded state, and independently inspected.

The current MVP demonstrates a narrow learning-competency scenario through:

**Evidence → Interpretation → Verification → Consensus → Resolution → Competency State → Attestation → Public Verification**

The exact behavior that has been implemented is defined by the source code, tests, current product contract, and [public evaluation documents](../../docs/evaluation/01_PRODUCT.md).

## What the MVP demonstrates

- A defined synthetic scenario with structured evidence.
- Separation between source evidence, AI interpretation, verification outcomes, consensus, resolution, and competency state.
- A bounded state representation whose meaning is constrained by the recorded evidence and resolution.
- A technical path for anchoring a defined record and checking its integrity.

## What remains unproven

- Commercial demand, a validated buyer, willingness to pay, recurring adoption, ROI, or product-market fit.
- Statistical or operational independence of the verification mechanisms.
- Universal competency assessment, truth, merit, fairness, or fitness for every organizational workflow.
- Broad workforce, role-evolution, or cross-organization applications beyond the current implementation.

## Evidence discipline

A demo is not a pilot. A passing test suite is not market validation. An attestation proves properties of a defined record; it does not independently establish that the underlying competency claim is true.

This public thesis is deliberately limited to the current MVP and does not serve as a roadmap or substitute for executable behavior.
